1. Ready to Learn

Workflow
     Strong learners are organised. They know where their work is, how to access it and how to submit it.

Set up your learning workflow

Avoid the common mistake

         Do not rely on Desktop or Downloads as your only copy. If your work is not saved properly, you risk losing it.

2. Control Your MacBook

Efficiency
     Efficient learners reduce friction. Your MacBook should work for you.

     You do not need to use every feature. The goal is to choose gestures and shortcuts that make your learning faster,      calmer and more effective.

Adapt your gestures

         Find these settings here: System Settings → Trackpad

Two-finger scroll

Move quickly through pages and documents.

Three-finger swipe

Switch spaces or full-screen apps efficiently.

Pinch to zoom

Read detail more clearly and work precisely.

Build shortcut fluency

         Find more shortcuts here: System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts

         Choose your personal top five. You are not expected to memorise everything at once.

3. Thinking with AI: Flint

AI Literacy
     Flint is our approved AI learning tool. It is designed to make you think hard — and when you are thinking hard, you are learning.

Use Flint to support thinking

Do not use Flint to avoid thinking

         If Flint is doing the thinking, you are not learning.
Good use Poor use
“Quiz me on this topic.” “Write my homework.”
“Challenge my argument.” “Make it sound like I did more than I did.”
“Explain this another way.” “Give me a final answer to submit.”

4. Safe, Responsible and Respectful

Digital Citizenship
     You are as responsible for your actions online as you are in real life.

Core expectations

The “stand over it” test

Before you post, message or share something, ask:

  • Could I stand over this if it were read aloud to adults?
  • Could I stand over this if it were screenshotted?
  • Could I stand over this if it were shared more widely?
  • Could I stand over this if there were serious consequences?

5. Look After the Device

Care & Security

Daily care check

If something goes wrong

         Do not hide the problem and do not try to fix it yourself.
  • Damage: report it to your Form Tutor and IT Service Desk.
  • Loss: tell a trusted adult immediately.
  • Settings issue: raise an IT ticket.
  • Online worry: tell a teacher or safeguarding lead.

6. Balance Matters

Wellbeing
     Quality of learning matters more than time on device.

Stay focused

         Your MacBook should help you learn more, not less. If it becomes a distraction, change the setup or ask for support.

Purposeful use over screen time

         Use your MacBook when it improves the quality of your learning. Put it away when another approach is better.
